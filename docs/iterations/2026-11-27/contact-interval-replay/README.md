## Compare the same time interval and coordinate frame

At control time `t`, the observer's unfiltered `interval_mean` describes
`[t−5ms,t]`. The old truth contact summary describes the final1ms solve.
They need not have the same contact family, force or moment.

For each completed1ms solve, map its full reported point forces using the
Jacobian at that solve's preceding pose. Integrate the native generalized
components first, then rotate only the free-base angular components with the
observer's single interval-midpoint rotation:

```text
Q_interval = W(R_mid) sum_j [h_j sum_c J_native(q_before_j,p_jc)^T F_jc] / 5ms
```

Repeat at the post-solve poses as a timing-sensitivity control. Compare force,
base moment and joint-load vectors separately; their units differ. The table
shows the RMS norm of the three joint-load errors, in N m.

## Two different mechanisms at the difficult events

**32.110s: the contact actually changes within those5ms.** The first three
samples carry shellB normal load near17.9N; the final two carry beltB load.
The last sample therefore looks belt-only. The raw observer still correctly
contains the preceding shell impulse: shellB joint load is `+0.016193 N m`,
versus `+0.016228 N m` from integrated shell forces and zero from the last solve.
This is temporal transfer, not evidence of simultaneous two-contact support or
CPU computation delay. A similar interval/end mismatch occurs in the good
7.875s control.

**35.205s: positive-pressure labels hide a force-bearing shell record.** BeltB
is the only family with positive normal pressure in all five samples. However,
admitted shellB records in the third and fifth samples have reported tangential
forces of26.53N and12.72N with exactly zero normal component. Their depths are
positive, approximately1.01µm and2.29µm, and the normals point inward correctly.
Their integrated shellB torque is `−0.206606 N m`; the observer reports
`−0.206627 N m`. The difference is about`2.12e−5 N m`.

The source path explains how this disappears from the old family label:

1. The bridge publishes the full point force and admission flag.
2. `GazeboPlant.contact_records()` retains these admitted, positive-depth rows.
3. `measure_records()` includes their full force/moment in its net wrench.
4. Family normal loads, weighted centroids and contact counts use positive
   `F·normal`; these rows contribute zero to that pressure-based summary.

Our initial interpretation “all five steps are beltB” was therefore only true
of **positive normal pressure**, not all reported force routing. The archived
correction preserves that distinction. Earlier dominant-normal-load error counts
remain historical results against that label; they cannot automatically be read
as errors against all dynamically active material bodies. We have not recomputed
I-60–I-67 labels or error rates from this one-phase replay.

Tangential force with zero normal force needs a solver/contact-boundary audit.
If correctly paired, those components are incompatible with ideal pointwise
Coulomb friction. The current source inspection
does not establish whether the installed solver, convergence, contact handling
or reporting boundary produces it. Do not call it physically valid friction,
normal tension, a flipped normal, observer hallucination or a proven DART bug.

A separately registered **posthoc** row audit finds1,119 such admitted records
in290 of8,000 primary intervals. Its numerical detection rule is
`abs(Fn) <= 1e-10*max(1,|F|)` and `|Ft| > 1e-8 N`; this identifies numerical
zero normal projection, not a learned physical threshold. The largest tangent
force is38.04N; rejected rows contribute none. The pattern also appears in the
good7.875s event, so its presence alone does not explain a trajectory failure.
[Separate posthoc report](evidence/analysis/posthoc_zero_normal_stage01/report.json).

## What the velocity and normal checks show

The material-velocity calculation uses actual solver points/normals and measured
q/v, with the projected commanded belt surface motion. It retains the norm of
the pressure-weighted mean vector, the pressure-weighted RMS speed and the
largest individual positive-load speed. RMS avoids cancellation between contacts.

At7.875s the pre/post-pose interval slip RMS is0.04088/0.000329m/s; at32.110s it
is0.01505/0.000281m/s. At35.205s it remains0.13261/0.13921m/s. Thus the later
event has a substantial rolling-consistency mismatch even at post-solve state.
The intrinsic-normal prior differs from the pressure-weighted interval normal
by about7.24° there, versus1.93° at32.110s. The live filtered normal is a separate
quantity, shown in the appendix.

This supports examining the zero-slip and single-point assumptions. It does not
prove that one likelihood term caused the tracking deviation, identify a new
optimal weight or justify using simulator truth in the estimator. In particular,
we should understand the unusual contact forces before fitting weights around
their behavior.

## Decision

Keep native LQR, momentum observer, geometry locator, feedforward and preparation.
The next target is a small controlled audit of the shell/belt contact forces
and pressure-based reference labels, followed by an interval-consistent contact
model. Revisit the saved candidate ranking with corrected evaluation semantics
before another adaptive threshold, particle filter or controller change.
This iteration identifies why an apparently wrong observer load can be right;
it does not yet improve the robot's tracking.
