# I-69 native capture result

The zero-normal/nonzero-tangent force in the old 35.205 s event comes from the
actual installed Dantzig solution and is applied to the shell contact. It is
not introduced by the bridge's force/normal pairing or invented by the momentum
observer. This is consistent with Dantzig's documented approximation of setting
friction bounds once from an earlier normal solution.

The diagnostic replay preserves all 59 precision arrays and all 36 I68 trace
arrays byte for byte, according to the parent parity checks. The private
interposer delegates the installed calls unchanged. This analysis checks the
original arrays and directly recorded applied impulses; it changes no solve,
controller or estimator.

## Exact association

The four fixed intervals contain 20 Dantzig calls, all returning success. PGS is
never called. There are 140 applied contact records. Each applied impulse equals
its original-order parent solver solution segment exactly. Reconstructing force
from normal, tangent basis, impulse and step duration differs from the recorded
DART force by at most 3.55e-15 N.

All 140 applied contacts match both the new bridge trace and the unchanged I68
trace. All 25 nonzero-force records have an unambiguous family match. Matched
force and normal errors are zero; maximum point difference is 2.29e-13 m.
Body-order sign is handled explicitly.

At the interval ending **35.205 s**, two shell-B contacts have these applied
impulses (normal, tangent 1, tangent 2), in N s:

| Physics substep | Parent solve | Original normal index | Applied impulse | Final tangent limits |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 3 | 138 | 1 | (0, 0.0201248655, -0.0172934405) | (0, 0) |
| 5 | 152 | 1 | (0, 0.0104874728, -0.0072003873) | (0, 0) |

Both parent calls return success. Original tangent rows 2 and 3 reference normal
row 1. The actual applied tangent impulses exceed the limits obtained by
recomputing `mu*abs(final normal impulse)`. These produce exactly the shell-B
forces of 26.5344 N and 12.7213 N found in I68. Their time-averaged joint load
explains the old momentum-observer shell-B component.

This is stronger than observing a large circular friction norm: these contacts
fail the individual component bounds themselves. The effect cannot be excused
by replacing a circular Coulomb cone with a two-tangent friction pyramid.

## Fixed controls

Across the four intervals, 12 of 140 applied contacts exceed final-dependent
component bounds under the frozen numerical screen. That screen is a floating
point diagnostic, not physical calibration.

- **7.875 s:** two rim-A contacts in substep 2 have zero normal and nonzero
  tangent impulses. The good-case label therefore does not exclude this effect.
- **32.110 s:** none of the five solves has a final-bound violation. The
  earlier-shell/later-belt interval history remains the explanation identified
  by I68 for the endpoint-inactive shell load.
- **35.055 s:** four belt-B contacts exceed a tangent component bound, and one
  shell-B contact has zero normal with nonzero tangent impulse.
- **35.205 s:** three belt-B contacts exceed a tangent component bound, together
  with the two zero-normal shell-B contacts above.

The controlled SPD probe independently reproduces the mechanism in the
installed library. Dantzig returns `(normal=0,tangent=.7)` while satisfying its
frozen box. Default PGS fails its own convergence/complementarity check, so this
does not justify simply switching the live solver to PGS.

## Interpretation and next decision

An endpoint's positive normal-load family is an incomplete description of the
forces generated by this simulation. It can label “belt B only” while the solver
also applies a shell-B tangent force. A single-belt point-force model has zero
shell-B joint authority regardless of point choice; moving that point cannot
repair this missing force route.

Retain the original LQR, momentum observer and feedforward for now. This result
does not establish that the friction approximation caused the large trajectory
deviation or all contact-selection errors. It does establish the cause of the
specific zero-normal/tangent rows and explains why some earlier comparisons
misattributed loads to observer/model error. Real hardware evidence is absent.

The next proposed work should target a contact solver/model with verified
final-dependent force bounds and preserve the native result as the baseline.
Any alternative requires its own convergence checks and controlled comparison
before trajectory conclusions or estimator-weight changes. No such alternative
has been implemented or adopted by this audit.
